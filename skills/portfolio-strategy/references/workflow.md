# Strategy mapping and review

## Sources and taxonomy

Start with supplied strategy, product plans, organization charts, roster/contact lists, invention disclosures, and portfolio exports. Record accessible source locators. Do not imply that an unreadable attachment was reviewed. Distinguish source statements, confirmed user decisions, analyst inferences, and proposals.

Prefer technology names an engineering leader recognizes. Define what belongs inside and outside a bucket. Avoid using only a broad label such as "AI" when the business can identify a concrete product capability. Allow a record to map to multiple buckets with explicit rationale; bucket totals consequently are not additive.

For unresolved ownership, retain an empty team assignment and ask who owns the feature. Keep parent/child organization relationships and shared team participation explicit. The analyzer validates organization cycles but does not infer that a person in a child team belongs to every parent bucket. A roster association is not an inventorship determination or a contribution history.

## Strategy targets

Capture the feature, jurisdiction, horizon, priority, business objective, and source. A business objective may be product, offensive, or defensive. Capture actual competitor evidence separately in the narrative when supplied; do not infer competitor use or infringement from the objective label.

The desired_family_count is an optional planning concept represented by a nonnegative integer in the input (use zero if no numerical target is set and explain this convention in the source). The known-family shortfall compares a configured count with confirmed mapped active family labels in that jurisdiction. It does not establish feature coverage, novelty, useful breadth, or the number of new applications required. Unknown family assignments can inflate that apparent shortfall. Pending and issued records are both included in this planning count but remain separate in inventory status counts.

## Strategy-gap review

Use separate layers:

1. **Inventory signal:** no confirmed mapped records, pending candidates only, inactive candidates only, unknown status, or issued candidates requiring review.
2. **Reported counsel assessment:** a supplied supports/partial/gap/unknown result, with feature/jurisdiction, record scope, reviewer, rationale, date, and source references.
3. **Refresh state:** whether the supplied assessment and its referenced records meet the company's configurable review-window convention.

A window of 180 days in the example is arbitrary company policy, not a legal standard. A within-window source is not independently verified. A stale support assessment is not converted to a gap. A current recorded gap remains limited to its source scope, not proof of an absence throughout the company or outside the dataset.

Support/partial entries require issued records with claim locators. This enforces data prerequisites, not legal truth: the software does not read or evaluate claims. A manually supplied gap may reference no records but must have a rationale and source that explains the review scope. Do not invent counsel approval to make a report look complete.

## Review actions

- Reconcile unmapped records, proposed classifications, missing families, and duplicate identifiers.
- Confirm unknown team ownership and outdated organization assignments.
- Refresh undated/old status evidence and stale reviews.
- Retrieve relevant claims and technical context for counsel review.
- Review open disclosures against targets, without counting filed/declined disclosure rows as open pipeline or as portfolio records.
- Send confirmed priorities into filing review and budgeting with actual costs and legal timing review.

No automatic action is authorized by a count, priority, assessment flag, or affordable budget scenario.
